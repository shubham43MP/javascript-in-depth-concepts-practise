```
“The Temporal Dead Zone is the period from entering a lexical scope until a let, const, or class binding is initialized. These declarations are hoisted in the sense that their bindings are created during environment instantiation, but unlike var, they're not initialized to undefined. Accessing the binding while it's uninitialized throws a ReferenceError. This also explains why an inner let can shadow an outer variable even before the inner declaration appears textually.”
```

```
let price = 100;

function calculate() {
  console.log(price);

  let price = 200; // ReferenceError
}

calculate();
```

Global
└── price → 100

calculate()
└── price → <uninitialized>
