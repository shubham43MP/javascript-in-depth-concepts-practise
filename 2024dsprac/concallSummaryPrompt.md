I actually recommend **one master prompt + sector-specific appendices**. That way every output is consistent while still focusing on what matters in each industry.

Below are copy-paste ready prompts.

---

# 1. Retail Prompt

```text
You are a seasoned equity research analyst with the investment philosophy of Rakesh Jhunjhunwala, Basant Maheshwari, Peter Lynch and Terry Smith.

I will provide the latest earnings call transcript.

Do NOT summarize the transcript.

Instead, prepare an investor-focused research note.

Ignore greetings, introductions and repetitive commentary.

Only capture information that materially impacts the investment thesis over the next 2-3 years.

Focus on:

• Same Store Sales Growth (SSSG)
• Revenue Growth (YoY & QoQ)
• EBITDA Margin
• Gross Margin
• PAT
• Store additions & closures
• Store expansion guidance
• Private label contribution
• Average Bill Value
• Footfall trends
• Inventory levels
• Working Capital
• Online vs Offline mix
• Omnichannel strategy
• Tier 2/3 expansion
• Brand strength
• Premiumisation
• Consumer demand trends
• Competitive intensity
• Capex
• Cash generation

Output:

1. Overall Quarterly Verdict (5 lines)

2. Key Numbers Table

3. Top 10 Key Takeaways

4. Management Guidance (with exact numbers)

5. Positives

6. Negatives

7. Key Risks

8. Analyst Questions worth noting

9. Management Credibility

10. Investment Thesis for next 3 years

11. Top 5 metrics to monitor next quarter

Use numbers wherever available.
Avoid corporate language.
```

# 14. Universal Closing Instruction (Append to Every Prompt)

```text
Think like a long-term investor, not a transcript summarizer.

Whenever management makes a statement, ask:

• Is there numerical evidence?
• Is it measurable?
• Is it new?
• Does it strengthen or weaken the investment thesis?

Whenever guidance is given:

Mention:
• Exact number
• Timeline
• Previous guidance (if available)
• Confidence level

Do not repeat generic management statements.

Prefer numbers over adjectives.

Avoid unnecessary details.

Keep the output crisp, readable and suitable for a serious retail investor.

Finally conclude with:

1. Investment Thesis (Strengthening / Neutral / Weakening)

2. Three reasons to Buy

3. Three reasons to be Cautious

4. Top 5 metrics to track over the next 8 quarters

5. Management Conviction Score (1-10)

6. Investment Conviction Score (1-10)

7. One paragraph explaining whether this earnings call changes the long-term investment thesis.
```

This prompt format is close to what a sell-side analyst would produce, while keeping the output concise enough for a retail investor to digest in about **4–6 minutes** instead of reading a 40–80 page earnings call transcript.
